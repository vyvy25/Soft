# Competitor References

Direct competitors to study for positioning, design patterns, and messaging. Use these to understand the landscape — not to copy, but to find the gaps Market Pay can own.

---

## Tazapay
**URL:** https://tazapay.com

B2B cross-border payments platform focused on Asia. Enables businesses to collect and pay internationally using local payment methods. Their site is the closest visual and navigation reference for Market Pay — clean layout, sticky mega-menu header, content-rich footer.

**What to watch:**
- Navigation structure and mega menu patterns
- How they present trust signals and compliance
- Messaging tone for B2B fintech audiences

---

## Payment Asia
**URL:** https://paymentasia.com/en/

Hong Kong-based payment gateway serving merchants across Asia. Covers online payments, payment links, POS terminals, and a wide range of regional payment methods. PCI DSS compliant with rates starting at 1.2% and a unified merchant dashboard.

**What to watch:**
- How they handle a wide product range without overwhelming the page
- Regional payment method presentation (wallets, cards, local rails)
- Pricing communication style

---

## Verto
**URL:** https://verto.co/

B2B cross-border payments and FX platform serving businesses across 190+ countries. Covers multi-currency accounts, international transfers, payroll, and treasury. Claims ~40% cost reduction on cross-border payments with local account access and competitive FX rates.

**What to watch:**
- How they communicate FX savings with concrete numbers
- Multi-currency account positioning
- Messaging for startups vs. enterprise audiences

## How to use the reference screenshots

Reference screenshots are stored in `3-assets/reference-website-screenshot/`. Before building any page section, open the  screenshots and review it against the three areas below. These are not style rules — they are a checklist to run before you start writing HTML.

---

### 1. Layout

Study each section in the reference for its structural pattern, not its content.

- **Borrow structures freely.** If a reference page solves the same communication problem with a clear layout (stats strip, alternating text/visual rows, feature grid, dark emphasis section), use the same structure. The content will be different — the bones can be the same.
- **Patterns to look for:** hero with text left and product visual right; trust strip immediately below hero; alternating image/text rows to break rhythm; dark section mid-page as a visual anchor; 3–4 card grid for features; full-width case study block.
- **Check the rhythm.** Good pages alternate between dense and open, dark and light, full-width and constrained. If three light sections follow each other, insert a dark or full-bleed break.

---

### 2. Illustrations

Tazapay uses lightweight, product-grounded visuals — not heavy custom illustration. Match this approach.

- **Build a realistic product UI mockup yourself** using HTML and CSS — a checkout form, a dashboard card, a transaction list, whatever fits the section. Place it in a clean floating card or implied browser frame. Do not use abstract shapes or icon-heavy illustrations as the main section visual. The mockup does not need to be functional, but it must look like something a real product would show.
- **Background washes:** place UI mockups on a soft pastel or brand-tinted background (light mint, light peach, light purple). Never put a mockup on a plain white background — it floats.
- **Floating metric badges:** layer small data cards or status badges over the main visual. Examples: a green "Authorized" badge, a currency conversion mini-card, a stat ("70% conversion uplift"). These make the illustration feel alive and product-specific.
- **Keep it light.** The visual should support the copy, not compete with it. If it needs a lot of explanation, it is too complex.

---

### 3. Images

Use real human photographs in sections that require trust — testimonials, case studies, solution hero banners.

- **People build trust.** A photo of a real person (customer, employee, business owner) converts faster than any icon or abstract visual. Use it in testimonials, case study callouts, and industry solution heroes.
- **Match the industry.** Travel pages use photos of airports or leisure travelers. Fintech pages use professionals at a desk. Gaming pages use players. Pick images that a reader in that industry would recognize immediately.
- **Treat the image.** Apply a soft rounded corner, a subtle overlay, or a partial crop. Never drop a raw rectangular photo directly into the layout.
- **Faces near CTAs.** Put testimonials or "trusted by" blocks close to a conversion action. Social proof works best right before you ask someone to do something.
